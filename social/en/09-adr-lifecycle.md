# 09 · Architecture decisions need a lifecycle

Reference article: `/en/docs/adr`

---

A one-page ADR can save your team months of "why did we do it this way?"

What makes an ADR useful isn't the what, it's the why:

- **Context:** facts and constraints, without defending an option.
- **Options considered:** at least two, including the conservative one.
- **Decision** in active voice: "We chose…".
- **Consequences:** the good and the bad.

It also has states: proposed, accepted, rejected, deprecated or superseded. A decision that no longer applies isn't deleted: a new ADR replaces it and explains the change.

My challenge: on your next big PR, attach a one-page ADR. If you can't write its options, you probably haven't compared them.

What decision in your project would nobody be able to justify today?
