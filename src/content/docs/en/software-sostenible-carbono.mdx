# Sustainable software: footprint as an attribute

All software consumes electricity, and electricity carries a carbon cost that changes with place and time. So the footprint of a
system is not a fixed property: it depends on how much energy each operation uses and which grid powers it at that moment. Treating
it as a quality attribute means measuring it, attributing it to concrete decisions and comparing alternatives, just as you would
latency or cost.

## What to measure

The most useful approach multiplies two figures: the **energy** a unit of work consumes, in kWh, and the **carbon intensity** of the
grid where and when it runs, in grams of CO₂ equivalent per kWh. Hardware's embodied emissions add to that, though they are harder to
allocate.

The _Software Carbon Intensity_ (SCI) specification, published as ISO/IEC 21031:2024, expresses the result per **functional unit**: per
request, per active user or per transaction. That choice matters, because a total can fall simply because the service has fewer users.

There is no universal factor to copy. Grid intensity depends on country, generation mix and time of day, and it varies with the source
you use. Document which source you used and when you checked it.

## Where it comes from

The footprint tends to concentrate in a few sources. Idle compute: oversized servers and test environments left on all day. Redundant
work: repeated queries, missing caches, jobs that recompute what has not changed. Unused data: logs and copies that nobody reads. And
transfers: moving data between regions consumes energy and often goes unnoticed.

## Levers

There are four levers, roughly in order of typical effect. **Reduce the work**: fewer cycles per request, caching and minimal data.
**Retire what nobody uses**: environments, copies and logs without consumers. **Adjust usage**: scale idle components to zero and batch
work that is not urgent. **Choose region and time**: shift flexible load to regions or hours with a cleaner grid.

The first two carry no technical trade-off. The last one does: a cleaner region may be further from users, which raises latency, and it
can clash with data residency rules.

## A worked example in numbers

A weekly report consumes 40 kWh per run. In region A the grid is at 400 gCO₂e/kWh: 40 × 400 = 16,000 g, or 16 kg per run. Moving the report
to a nightly batch in region B, at 150 gCO₂e/kWh, brings it down to 6 kg. If the work is also cut by 25 % (skipping recalculation of data
that has not changed), consumption drops to 30 kWh and the footprint to 4.5 kg. The figures are illustrative, not official factors; the
point is that the region change and the compute change add up, and each can be measured on its own.

## Measuring without inventing numbers

Use the provider's measurements where they exist, and documented estimates where they do not. Report ranges rather than falsely precise
figures, and record the method: what the estimate includes, what it leaves out and which grid-intensity source was used. A figure without a
method cannot support a decision.

## Common mistakes

- **Trusting a declared neutrality.** Buying certificates says nothing about the grid the system draws from in each hour. Look at the real
  intensity at the time.
- **Measuring only compute.** Storage, copies and network traffic also use energy.
- **Optimising one metric at the cost of another.** Moving everything to the cleanest region can worsen latency and transfer costs.
- **Treating it as a marketing initiative.** If it does not appear in design reviews, nothing changes.

## When it does not apply

The principle always applies, but the depth of measurement should be proportionate. An internal tool that runs once a month does not
justify a detailed measurement system, though it does justify not leaving it running for no reason.

## Checklist

- [ ] Do you know how much energy a unit of work of the service consumes?
- [ ] Is the footprint expressed per functional unit, with its grid-intensity source documented?
- [ ] Are there environments, copies or logs without consumers?
- [ ] Can flexible load move to a cleaner hour or region without breaking other requirements?
- [ ] Does the footprint appear in design reviews alongside cost and latency?
