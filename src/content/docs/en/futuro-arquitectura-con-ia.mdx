# Architecture with AI: what to bet on, what to watch

Nobody knows what AI will look like in 2027. What we do know is what can already be measured,
what is a reasonable assumption, and which decisions can be undone if the assumption fails. This
article keeps those three layers apart. The thesis is simple: **bet heavily only on what is
observable, treat hypotheses as hypotheses with signals that confirm or disprove them, and take
today the reversible decisions that leave room to correct course**.

This is not an article of predictions. Every factual claim comes with a source and a date; every
assumption is labeled as one. [Lock-in](/docs/casado-a-un-proveedor) and
[control of autonomy](/docs/controlar-la-ia-gobierno-y-autonomia) already have their own articles,
so here we look at what changes on the horizon and how to decide without depending on it.

<Callout tone="info" title="Cut-off date">
  The figures are from October 2026. Several come from analyst reports or from the providers
  themselves, and that is noted when it applies. Before deciding on a number, open the original
  source: figures in this field go stale within months.
</Callout>

## Why predicting is not enough

A strategy that depends on one concrete prediction ("in 2027 agents will do X") fails twice: if
the prediction is wrong, and if, even when right, it arrives on a different schedule. A strategy
that separates what is already true from what is likely can move without being rewritten.

There is a common trap: confusing the speed of a phenomenon with its destination. Cost per
capability falling very fast says nothing about whether the same providers will still be around,
nor whether an organization's total spend will fall. It is worth reading every trend with that
distinction in mind.

## What can already be verified

### Agents in production, with more caution than enthusiasm

As the author's judgment, agentic AI projects tend to stall for three reasons: rising costs, unclear
business value and insufficient risk controls. This is a reading frame, not a figure: the article
cites no cancellation forecast, because the analyst's primary source could not be opened.

What is observable is protocol adoption and the existence of deployments, covered in the next
sections.

### Interoperability protocols: MCP and A2A

- **MCP (Model Context Protocol).** Anthropic donated it on 9 December 2025 to the Agentic AI
  Foundation, a foundation co-founded by Anthropic, Block and OpenAI.
  According to the announcement itself, there are more than 10,000 active public MCP servers and
  more than 97 million monthly downloads of the Python and TypeScript SDKs. These are figures from
  the protocol's own provider.
- **A2A (Agent2Agent).** According to a Linux Foundation press release dated 9 April 2026, its
  specification now has version 1.0, the first stable one. The same release speaks
  of more than 150 supporting organizations and production use across several sectors; these too
  are claims from the promoters.

What is verifiable is that interoperability is already governed beyond any single provider. What a
protocol does **not** guarantee is that two tools are interchangeable: the schema is shared, but
permissions, data and the meaning of each integration remain yours to define. That reading is the
author's judgment.

### Cost per capability falls; total spend not necessarily

On 22 September 2026 Epoch AI published "The plunging price of thought". Its estimate: the cost of
reaching a fixed performance level has fallen about 47% per quarter since 2023, roughly 13 times a
year. It carries caveats that matter more than the headline:

- Near the frontier the decline is much faster right after a level is first reached, and it slows
  over time. According to its analysis across five benchmarks, the cost at state of the art falls
  about 75 times a year and, two years later, about 4.7 times a year.
- The calculation assumes users always pick the cheapest model for each task. Epoch notes
  that real users do not switch models that often, so real savings would be smaller.

Separately, on 5 October 2026 Epoch AI reported that coding-agent spending by OpenAI researchers,
valued at API prices, is doubling roughly every month. This is an exponential fit to preliminary
OpenAI data, with estimated doubling times of 27 to 34 days depending on the group. Putting both together, the reading we hold
(author's judgment) is this: unit prices fall very fast, but if usage grows faster than efficiency,
the bill goes up. Inference cost is covered in depth in [inference cost](/docs/costo-de-inferencia).

### Regulation in motion: the European calendar

The European Commission lists the current timeline of the AI Act:

- Obligations for general-purpose AI models apply from 2 August 2025.
- Transparency rules apply from August 2026.
- The Digital Omnibus on AI entered into force on 27 July 2026, after a political agreement on 7
  May. It postpones high-risk use cases in Annex III to 2 December 2027, and those in Annex I to 2
  August 2028.

The delay changes when compliance is required, not whether it is. A system that makes decisions
about people (employment, credit, education) will have to demonstrate what it did, with which data,
and who supervised it, and that evidence takes months to build.

## Reasonable hypotheses, not verified

The following are **assumptions**. Each has a signal that would confirm it and one that would
disprove it; without those signals they are not useful for deciding.

- **H1. Capability gets cheaper, but the frontier stays expensive.** The advantage stops being
  access to a model and becomes having evaluations, data and processes that use it well. It
  conflicts with the slower frontier decline Epoch describes, so it is not a certainty.
- **H2. Protocols consolidate and the difficulty moves elsewhere.** The question will no longer be
  which protocol to use, but who authorizes an agent, how it is identified across organizations and
  how its actions with third-party tools are audited.
- **H3. Agents take bounded, reversible tasks with human review.** Long tasks, those without clear
  metrics or with a high cost of error will stay with people, and many projects will be abandoned
  for lack of measurable value. It has no date of its own.
- **H4. Traceability is required by contract before it is required by law.** Customers, especially
  those operating in the EU, will ask for records of AI decisions in their tenders long before the
  legal calendar obliges them to.

## Signals that confirm or disprove each hypothesis

| Hypothesis | Signal that would confirm it                                  | Signal that would disprove it                                    | Where to look                                  |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| H1         | Cost per task drops two quarters in a row for your use case   | A new frontier model raises your cost per task with no real gain | Your cost-per-task series; Epoch AI data       |
| H2         | Several large vendors implement MCP and A2A in their products | A new closed protocol displaces the open ones                    | Linux Foundation changelogs and press releases |
| H3         | Agent cases in production with published value metrics        | Growing cancellations in analyst surveys                         | Analyst reports with explicit methodology      |
| H4         | Tenders asking for decision traces with concrete clauses      | Customers who ask nothing about traceability for a year          | Your own tenders and those of your sector      |

Two reading rules. First, one isolated signal decides nothing: look for the one that repeats across
several quarters. Second, if the source is the vendor itself or an analyst who sells tools, weigh
it accordingly.

## Reversible decisions for today

Reversibility is the central criterion for deciding under uncertainty. Decisions in this group share
one property: if the hypothesis fails, correcting them costs weeks, not years.

- **Your own layer between the application and the model provider.** A company interface with one
  adapter per provider, and the most sensitive calls isolated. It is the typical example of
  [portability by layers](/docs/casado-a-un-proveedor#portability-layer-by-layer).
- **Your own versioned evaluations.** A set of real cases with acceptance criteria that runs every
  time you change model or prompt. It serves every hypothesis, because it is what measures their
  signals. See [continuous evaluation](/docs/rag-y-evaluacion-continua).
- **Traces for every agent decision.** Inputs, tools invoked, model version and outcome, stored in a
  format that does not depend on a specific dashboard. It is the basis of the audit regulation asks
  for and of any future analysis.
- **An inventory of data and permissions.** What data exists, who can let an agent use it, and with
  what classification. It is paid once and works with any model.
- **Open protocols at the edges.** Exposing your internal capabilities as MCP-compatible tools is a
  thin wrapper that is easy to remove. It is a cheap choice, not a commitment to a vendor.

## Bets worth postponing

These decisions are hard to undo and depend on hypotheses not verified today. This does not mean
avoiding them forever; it means making them once the signals have arrived.

- **Multi-year consumption contracts with a single agent vendor**, especially if price or billing
  model can change before the contract ends.
- **Redesigning a whole process around one specific agent** without evaluations that show what
  happens when that agent changes.
- **Training or hosting your own model at scale** before you have the volume and evaluations to
  justify it. The safe path goes through [taking a model to production](/docs/mlops-modelo-a-produccion)
  before investing in infrastructure.
- **Reorganizing roles or headcount** based on a forecast of task substitution. It is the most
  expensive decision to reverse and the one with the fewest verified signals.

## How to review the strategy every quarter

A strategy with a horizon is not written once. It is reviewed at a fixed rhythm, with the same
questions, so that changes show up as data rather than opinions.

A hypothesis register is enough to start. This is an illustrative shape, not a mandatory template:

```yaml
# shape of a hypothesis register (illustrative)
id: H1
statement: cost per task at fixed performance keeps falling
signal_confirms: cost per task drops two quarters in a row for our use case
signal_disproves: a new frontier model with no gain and higher cost
source: Epoch AI price data and our cost-per-task invoice
review: 2027-Q1
if_confirmed: route more tasks to the cheaper model
if_disproved: keep the own layer and rewrite nothing
```

In each review, in order:

1. Look at the signals for each hypothesis and update its status: confirmed, disproved or open.
2. Review the reversible decisions: are they still cheap to change?
3. Decide which postponed bet is activated, if any has met its condition.
4. Record what changed and why, in the same format as an architecture decision
   ([ADR](/docs/adr)).

## When to bet heavily, and when not to

Betting heavily makes sense when the use case has its own metrics, data you control and a low exit
cost: the bet can be wrong, but the damage is bounded.

It does not make sense when the key capability changes every quarter without evaluations that would
detect it, when the dependency is contractual or about data, or when the decision affects people
without a review mechanism. In those cases caution is not slowness: it is the cost of not being
able to go back.

## Common mistakes

- **Presenting a forecast as a fact.** A figure from an analyst or a date from a regulatory calendar
  is not a certainty about the market.
- **Using secondary figures without opening the source.** Many AI figures circulate rewritten and
  with variations. Cite the original, or say you could not consult it.
- **Planning with today's price.** If cost per task changes every few months, an annual plan without
  review is obsolete before it closes.
- **Confusing unit cost with the invoice.** The first can fall while the second rises.
- **Expecting the standard to solve governance.** A shared protocol does not define who authorizes,
  what gets recorded or who answers for it.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every trend in the plan has a source and a date, and predictions are labeled as such.
- [ ] Every hypothesis has a signal that confirms it and one that disproves it.
- [ ] Reversible decisions (provider layer, evaluations, traces, data) are taken.
- [ ] Postponed bets have a written condition for activation.
- [ ] The quarterly review has an owner, a date and a change log.
- [ ] Total cost (the invoice) is tracked alongside unit cost.
- [ ] The regulatory calendar that applies to your systems is identified with dates.
